## The one rule about pictures

**The only images that go on a slide are screenshots of the running software.** They are in
[`images/`](images/) - sixteen of them, listed in [`README.md`](README.md).

Everything else on every slide is **typed as native PowerPoint text**. Boxes, tables, bullets,
coloured panels - all of it, laid out with the template's own shapes.

This deck used to ship nine "infographic boards" that were pictures of text. They were sharper
than a typed slide at 100% and worse at every other size, they could not be searched, copied, or
fixed on the day, and a wrong word in one meant re-rendering an image. **If a thing is words, type
the words.**

The one exception is a **genuine diagram** - boxes with arrows between them, where the shape
carries meaning that a list does not. There are two of those here. The **architecture** is
already drawn, from real text at real pixels, and Slide 3 Block A says where the file is and
exactly how to rebuild it in PowerPoint shapes if you would rather. The **methodology ribbon**
has a generator prompt in [Part 2](#part-2---diagram-prompts). Even there: generate the shape
only, and put the text on top yourself.

### Block B - innovation and uniqueness

Section label, mono caps: `INNOVATION AND UNIQUENESS`. Four points, no more.

**These are capabilities, not caveats.** An earlier version of this block spent one of its four
slots on "honest about gaps", which is a limitation sitting in the place a judge looks for a
reason to shortlist you. Every limit this project takes seriously is still in the deck - the
coverage gap is on slide 5 as *where the next float should go*, the risks are slide 4 - but the
innovation block sells what the thing does.

**Write them the way the reader reads them: a short bold title, then two plain sentences.** The
rule for every line here is that somebody who has never opened an ocean tool can follow it. No
acronyms on this slide - the stack and the standards are on slide 3, where a technical reader is
already looking for them.

- **Fly inside the ocean.** Every other tool draws the sea as a flat map, or as one slice laid on
  a globe. This is a solid block of water you dive into and turn, from the surface down to
  **2000 metres**, so you see the shape of what is happening instead of guessing it from slices.
- **Every forecast comes with its error.** Click any float or buoy and see what it really measured
  next to what the model predicted, at every depth it reached. A forecaster knows which readings
  to trust before acting on them.
- **Cyclone fuel, in three dimensions.** The five things a cyclone forecaster asks about the
  water, worked out from INCOIS's own data and matching INCOIS's own published values **97% of the
  time**. Watch the warm layer build up and collapse across a year.
- **Works offline. Works with their tools.** No install, no login, no cost, and it keeps running
  if the internet drops - it opens on a venue laptop with the Wi-Fi off. The same data also comes
  out in the standard ocean formats, so software INCOIS already runs can read it.

**One closing line, if there is room:**

> **Nothing on this screen is guessed.** There is no model inventing water where nobody measured

### Block C - methodology, five stages

**Fetch &rarr; Check &rarr; Derive &rarr; Bake &rarr; Render and compare.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **01 Fetch** | Subset each source at the server, over one region and one window. Every endpoint tested and dated before it was trusted. |
| **02 Check** | Quality flags read per channel, so a float with a dead salinity sensor still gives its good temperature. Land masked, never back-filled into the sea. |
| **03 Derive** | Density from TEOS-10, the five cyclone-hazard fields (the depths checked against INCOIS's own archive), a temperature anomaly against a 1991-2020 normal, two observation counts, and unusual bodies of water located automatically. |
| **04 Bake** | Native grids for the API and the standards; byte volumes for the GPU; depth sheets and drapes left unquantised. **Committed to the repository**, so the demo needs no network. |
| **05 Render and compare** | Ray-march the block in the browser, draw every instrument where it truly was, score the model against each cast, and **publish the score**. |

### Block B - challenges met, and how

Five rows. Problem on the left, fix on the right, with the measurement that settled it.
**Write it in the past tense on purpose** - say out loud: *"this slide is a record, not a
forecast."*

| Risk we hit | Resolved |
| --- | --- |
| **The data disappears.** HYCOM and NOAA CoastWatch both timed out from our network on day one. INCOIS's own chlorophyll ends in 2006 and 2020, their currents and cyclone products in 2019, and the last glider left this basin on **14 Oct 2022**. | Every endpoint is tested, dated and written down, and the adapter seam makes a swap one class. We moved to INCOIS's own public ERDDAP, which made the demo **end-to-end INCOIS** instead of a substitute. |
| **The instruments lie.** One float here reported about **20 PSU** - fresher than the Baltic, impossible in this ocean - and the global quality standard let it through. | Two layers of quality control. Argo's own flags read per channel, so a float with a dead salinity sensor still gives its good temperature, then a regional floor catches what the global rule passes. Reading the flags properly **more than doubled** the usable observations. |
| **The hardware cannot cope.** A forecasting desk is not a gaming PC, and ray-marching every pixel of a 3D texture is heavy. | **4 bytes per voxel**, a ray-step slider, and textures freed as the animation plays. Built and tested on integrated graphics with 2 GB of shared memory. |
| **Missing data can lie.** A GPU blends neighbouring cells before the shader sees them, so blending "no data" into the sea paints a fake cold strip along every coast - and it looks exactly like real upwelling a forecaster would act on. | A separate channel records where the ocean actually is. Masked cells are back-filled from their nearest real neighbour so they are harmless to blend against, and **land can never contribute a value at any zoom**. |
| **A plausible wrong number**, which is worse than no number. A derived current field looked finite, physical and entirely convincing on screen. | Measured against reality, then deleted: it gave **0.16 m/s** for the Somali Current in peak monsoon against a real 1.5 to 2.5. Currents now come from Copernicus's own analysis, held to the same test and passing it: it puts the Somali Current core in the right place, at the right speed, in the month it peaks. |

> What changes for the person doing the work: **"the model looks off"** becomes *"warm by this
> much, at these depths, against this float's cast on this date"* - a number you can put in a
> report, and act on.

### How this differs from the tools that already exist

**What no other tool does:** put the model field and the in-situ profiles in the same picture, in
three dimensions, in a browser, and print the difference between them. Argovis serves Argo
profiles with no model field to compare them against. Copernicus MyOcean and the EU Digital Twin
Ocean render model fields, flat, with no instruments inside them. INCOIS's own Live Access Server
and ERDDAP give a map and a download. **Each is good at its own job. None of them answers "is the
model right, here, at this depth, today" - which is the question the problem statement is about.**

# WHAT NOT TO PUT ON ANY SLIDE

Each was considered and rejected for a reason.

| Do not | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Paste a picture of text** | It cannot be searched, copied, fixed on the day or read at a size other than the one it was made at. If it is words, type the words. Only screenshots go in as images. |
| Say "no other tool does this" | Argovis, Copernicus MyOcean and the EU Digital Twin Ocean have not all been checked. Say "co-visualisation in a browser, in 3D, with the comparison quantified" - the narrow claim is defensible, the absolute one is not. |
| Claim gliders, CTD sections, HF-radar or ADCP | None are in the build, and each was refused with a measurement. The last glider left this basin on 14 Oct 2022. Saying so is a stronger answer than a vague promise. |
| Claim machine learning anywhere | There is none, deliberately. A year of ten-day analyses is not a training set, and a neural gap-filler would paint over the coverage gap that is the most honest thing in the tool. |
| Quote an instrument count | Say **"instruments"**, not "Argo floats": the bake holds floats *and* moored buoys, the two are scored separately on purpose, and the number drawn at any one date is smaller than the number in the build. Every count here moves with a re-bake, so do not put one on a slide. |
| Say the currents are a picture | They **were**, and are not any more. ADR 0013 superseded 0011: they are Copernicus's own `uo` and `vo` at 1/12 degree, with a real speed under the cursor. The old caveat is now the opposite of the truth. |
| Call the moving current dots a simulation | They are dots carried by the analysed field on one depth, running the drift model's own step rule - a particle and a drift pin from the same start point end within metres of each other. Not three-dimensional, and refused as such: vertical velocity exists only as a model output nobody measured. ADR 0017. |
| Claim the platform predicts a drift track | It integrates the analysed currents and nothing else - no wind, no Stokes drift, no leeway. What makes it worth showing is that it is **scored against real floats**, so the line comes with a search radius. Read the figure off `FACTS.md` on the day; do not put it on a slide. |
| Add a stock photo of the ocean | Every picture in this deck is the real software. That is itself the argument. |
| Add a seventh slide | The portal caps it at six. |
| Say the platform runs on a phone | The **landing page** does, down to 320 px, and that is worth saying. The **console** is floored at 980 px and pans sideways below it, because the top bar alone is about 800 px. Say "any laptop", and hand the evaluator a laptop. |
| Put a coloured bar down the left of a row or a callout | The side stripe is banned throughout this project and all eight were removed from the product. A deck that reintroduces it is the only surface still doing it. Full borders or a background tint instead. |
