# SIH 2026 prototype video - script, version 2

**Written 2026-09-20.** It replaces [`script-v1-recorded.md`](script-v1-recorded.md), which is the
video already recorded and says several things the deck no longer does. **Re-record from this
file**, or the video linked on slide 6 contradicts slides 4 and 5.

**How to read this page.**

Plain text is what you say out loud. Read it as written.

> **On screen ·** Every indented grey line is an instruction and is **never spoken**. "On screen"
> lines are what you do, and each names a real control by its label in the app.

> **Note ·** Grey lines marked "Note" are directions to you, like this one. If a direction is not
> grey, it is a mistake in this file: make it grey rather than reading it aloud.

**Length:** about 900 spoken words. That is **5:40 at 160 words a minute**, plus the silences,
the camera moves and the landing-page opening: expect **about 6:10** recorded, and read it at 160
rather than dawdling. The trims below take roughly 90 words out, which brings it near 5:30.

**It tells the deck's story in the deck's order:** the problem, the one view, where the model is
wrong, the four uses the problem statement names (cyclones, search and rescue, fisheries,
climate), and why it runs anywhere.

**Numbers are kept to the few that do not move with an update**, and written the way they are
said: five metres to two kilometres, ninety-seven percent, a tenth, 1991 to 2020. No count of
instruments, variables or tests is spoken, because those change with every bake. Where a card or
panel shows a number, the viewer reads it off the screen.

**The audience is not technical.** A judge may never have opened an ocean model. No sentence uses
a word the picture has not already shown.

# Read this before you record

## Setting up

1. Open **https://rak2315.github.io/oceanverity/** - the landing page - and let the globe in its
   hero finish drawing. Open the platform once in a second tab so it is warm, and leave the
   landing page in front.
2. Record the opening **with the address bar visible**, so the live link is on screen. Everything
   from the dive onwards is full screen with **F11**, as a second clip, and the two are cut
   together. If you would rather not cut, stay windowed throughout.
3. In the left panel, close every group except **Variable**.
4. Record at **1920 x 1080**. Pick light or dark and keep it for the whole take.
5. Rehearse the dive, the storm walkthrough and the plankton float twice.
6. **Pick your plankton float before you record.** In the Biology tab with Chlorophyll selected,
   click float markers until the panel charts plankton rather than temperature. Only some floats
   carry that sensor. Remember which marker it was.
7. **Before the Wi-Fi moment**, every view you show must already have been opened once. The page
   fetches each view's data the first time it is opened, so once the Wi-Fi is off, only click
   floats. Do not change the variable or the date.

**Two words to say carefully.** **Thermocline** is "THERM-oh-cline". **Argo** is "AR-go".

## The five moments that have to land

Everything else supports them.

1. **The dive**, around 0:55. Let the camera move play out in full.
2. **The float comparison**, around 1:35. Stop moving the mouse and let people read the chart.
3. **The gap drawn as a gap**, around 2:10.
4. **The storm's warm-water sheet, then the heat falling along its path**, around 2:45 to 3:10.
5. **The closing montage**, from 5:20. It is the only place the whole build is seen at once, so
   cut it fast and let it run under the last lines.

## How to say it

**Pacing.** Say numbers slowly and in full: "ninety-seven percent", "nineteen ninety-one to
twenty twenty".

**Silences.** The two seconds before the first word and before the last two lines are deliberate.
Do not fill them.

**If you overrun**, lose these first, about seventy words together, without losing an argument:

- the Currents, Plankton, Temperature switch at 1:10 (keep the clicks, drop the sentence);
- "A cold wake behind it" and "An instrument that felt it" in the storm (click straight through);
- the plankton float in the fisheries section (keep the fronts and the oxygen floor);
- the second line of "The figure an oceanographer draws";
- the last sentence of "It runs anywhere".

**If a live take makes you nervous**, record the screen and the narration separately and lay the
audio over the footage. The actions are in order, so they can be captured in one pass.

## What not to say

Each of these was said in version 1 and is wrong or unprovable.

- "The model assimilates Argo." INCOIS grid the floats into a map; that is not assimilation.
- "The buoys it did not use." Nobody has confirmed the buoys were left out.
- "Five and a half times." The buoys are compared to 500 m and the floats to about 2000 m, so the
  two figures are not like for like.
- Any count of instruments, variables or tests. They change with every bake.
- "Drop your own file on the page." True, but it needs our server running and the live site has no
  server behind it. It is on the deck instead.

# The script

## 0:00 - 0:25 · The hook, on the live site

> **On screen ·** Open on the landing page, address bar visible. The hero reads "Fly into the
> Indian Ocean." over the ocean render. Two seconds of silence before the first word, then scroll
> slowly through one section while you speak.

India's ocean agency, INCOIS, publishes an analysis of the ocean around India. It is the kind of
information behind cyclone warnings, search and rescue, and fishing advisories.

And in that same water, robotic floats and moored buoys are measuring the real ocean, every day.

**This puts the two in one place**: the model as a block of water you fly into, the instruments
inside it where they actually were, and the gap between them given as a number.

> **On screen ·** Press the hero's button into the platform. It opens on the globe. If you are
> cutting two clips together, the full-screen one starts here.

---

## 0:25 - 0:45 · The problem

> **On screen ·** On the globe, press play at the left of the timeline and let the analyses run so
> the colours change.

This is how ocean data is usually seen. A flat map, one depth at a time, with the measurements in
a different tool.

> **On screen ·** Press play again to pause.

The data existed. What was missing was one place to see the two together, through the depth. So
the question that matters has no tool behind it. Not "what does the model say". **Is the model
right, here, at this depth, today?**

Tools exist for each half. Argo portals show you the floats. Model portals show you the model, as
a flat map. **None of them puts the two in one picture and tells you the difference.**

---

## 0:45 - 1:45 · One view, and the comparison

> **Note ·** The heart of the video. Do not rush it.

> **On screen ·** Click "Dive into the water", top right. Let the whole camera move play out.

Let me fly into it.

> **On screen ·** Drag slowly across the water once, so it is clearly three-dimensional.

That is the ocean around India as one body of water. **Five metres down to two kilometres, in a
browser tab.**

That sharp band is the thermocline, where warm surface water gives way to cold deep water. How
deep the warm layer goes decides how much fuel a cyclone can find.

> **On screen ·** In Variable, click the "Circulation" tab and "Current Speed", then the "Biology"
> tab and "Chlorophyll", then back to "Ocean state" and "Temperature". About a second on each.

Currents. Plankton. Temperature. Every variable in the same block of water.

> **On screen ·** Click one of the float markers near the top of the water. The comparison panel
> opens on the right. Stop moving the mouse for two full seconds.

And this is the part that matters. Those markers are real floats, drawn where they were on this
date. Click one.

**One line is what the float measured. The other is what the model predicted, at the same place
and the same depths.** The shaded band between them is the disagreement, and the panel puts a
number on it.

---

## 1:45 - 2:20 · Where the model is wrong, and where it is blind

> **On screen ·** Close the float panel. Open "Model vs instruments" and tick "Colour instruments
> by disagreement". Every marker recolours.

Now every instrument at once, coloured by how far the model sits from it.

INCOIS build their analysis from these floats, so the floats agree with it partly by design. The
moored buoys are a different network, so we keep their score separate. **They disagree more, and
we show that rather than average it away.**

> **On screen ·** Untick it. In Variable, click the "Evidence" tab and "Observation Coverage".

And this view shows where nobody has measured at all. About a tenth of this block has no
measurement behind it. Most tools quietly colour that in. **We draw the gap as a gap.**

---

## 2:20 - 3:25 · Cyclones, and search and rescue: a real storm

> **On screen ·** Click "Explore" in the top bar. Click "What did Cyclone Montha do to the ocean?"
> The first card opens, with IMD's track drawn in pink.

Here is a real storm. Cyclone Montha, in the Bay of Bengal, October 2025, on the Indian
Meteorological Department's official track.

> **On screen ·** Click "Next". Then "Next" again, to "How deep the warm water ran". A curved sheet
> appears inside the block. Rotate slowly so its shape shows.

Before it formed, the water along its path held a lot of heat. This sheet is where the water drops
below twenty-six degrees. **Where it dips, the warm layer runs deep**, and a storm cannot stir up
cold water to weaken itself.

On a flat map that is a number in a table. Here it is a shape you see at a glance.

And we did not simply draw this. INCOIS published this same field themselves, from 2004 to 2019.
We ran our code over those years and compared cell by cell: **the two agree in ninety-seven
percent of them.**

> **On screen ·** Click "Next" to "The storm drew the heat out". Let the viewer read the card.

Ten days later the heat along its path had fallen, while the same bay away from the path had
gained heat. **The drop follows the storm, not the season.**

> **On screen ·** Click "Next" through "A cold wake behind it" and "An instrument that felt it",
> a second on each, to "Where would something adrift go?". A drift line draws from a pin.

And if something goes into the water after a storm, where does it go? The pin rides the analysed
currents. **We tested that same method against where real floats actually drifted, so its error is
published rather than assumed.** Currents only, no wind, so it is a place to start a search, not a
rescue forecast.

---

## 3:25 - 4:20 · Fisheries

> **On screen ·** Close the card with its cross. In Variable, click the "Biology" tab, then
> "Surface Fronts". The layer paints on the sea surface. Rotate once.

Fishermen are the other half of this. INCOIS build their fishing advisories from **fronts**: lines
where warm water meets cool, or green water meets clear. Fish gather along them.

We work these out from satellite pictures of the sea surface, by the methods INCOIS name for their
own advisories. The darker the water here, the more of that cell sits on a front.

> **On screen ·** Click "Chlorophyll". The block fills with plankton, heaviest near the coast.

Underneath is the food. Plankton, through the whole column, not only at the top.

> **On screen ·** Click the float marker you rehearsed. The comparison panel opens, this time on
> plankton.

And the same check runs here. This float carries a plankton sensor, so the model is scored against
it exactly the way the temperature was.

> **On screen ·** Click "Oxygen Floor". A sheet appears inside the block. Rotate once.

Then the oxygen floor: the depth where the water runs out of the oxygen fish need. Above it there
is room to live. Below it there is not.

**These are the ingredients of an advisory, and we call them that.** The advisory itself stays
INCOIS's job.

---

## 4:20 - 4:40 · Climate

> **On screen ·** In Variable, click the "Change" tab and "Temperature vs Normal". Let it rotate.

And the long view. This is this year's ocean against the average from **1991 to 2020** for the
same month, at every depth. Red is warmer than normal, blue is cooler.

---

## 4:40 - 5:05 · The figure an oceanographer draws

> **On screen ·** Open "Vertical section" and press "Draw a line". Click once off the Somali
> coast and once in the Bay of Bengal. The slice draws under the panel. Let it sit for two
> seconds.

One more, for the people who do this for a living. Draw a line anywhere across the ocean and the
platform cuts the water along it, from the surface to the sea floor, with the real casts near
that line drawn on the same figure.

**It is cut in the browser, from the full-precision numbers**, not read off the picture on screen.

---

## 5:05 - 5:20 · It runs anywhere

> **On screen ·** Turn the Wi-Fi off on camera, then click a float marker and open its
> comparison. Do not change the variable or the date.

That is with the network off, by the way. Everything the page needs travels with it, so a dead
venue network cannot stop a demonstration. Nothing to install, no licence, and a new data source
is one small class.

---

## 5:20 - 5:55 · The close

> **Note ·** Turn the Wi-Fi back on before this, or shoot the montage as its own clip. It opens
> views the page has not fetched yet, and those need the network the first time.

> **On screen ·** A fast montage while these lines play, about half a second a cut: every Field
> button in the five Variable tabs, then "Set up a cyclone question" for the five cyclone fields,
> then the depth slice dragged down, the timeline scrubbed, the instruments coloured by
> disagreement, an anomaly ring, the guided tour card and the exhibition screen. End on the full
> block of water turning, with the live link on screen or as a caption, and two seconds of
> silence before the last two lines.

Every variable in the build, the depth, the time, the instruments, the odd water it finds by
itself, a guided tour and a screen for a public hall. One platform, one browser tab.

A cyclone forecaster, a search and rescue planner, a fisheries desk, a climate group and a school
class. Five people INCOIS already serves, one tool, and no new data collected for any of it.

Everything in this video is running right now, at that link. Open it, click any float, and check
our numbers yourself.
