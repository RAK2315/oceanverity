# `web/` - React + TypeScript + Three.js. One WebGL scene for globe and volume.

See the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) for the rules that govern this code, and
[`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md) for the vocabulary.

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | **The landing page.** No external request: tokens for both themes, a two-column hero with the headline left and a live WebGL globe right, a five-cell stat strip under it, seven sections, 14 feature cards that each open one shared `<dialog>` from `data-` attributes, four of whose measure lines are **rewritten from the manifest on load** - they were typed and three had gone stale (the drift score, the normal's cell count and the adapter count), so the typed line now carries no figure at all and names what the card measures instead; every other figure is fetched live from the same manifest the platform draws. The shell is 1400 px. **It sets Space Grotesk and Inter, not the console's Chivo and IBM Plex Mono** - the two halves of the product do not currently share a typeface, which is a live decision and not a settled one. The globe is `src/landingGlobe.ts`, and it and its orbit rings live in one `.globe-col` cell so the rings are concentric with it by construction - they were anchored to `right: 6%` of the hero and drifted **276 px** off the globe by 1200 px, cutting through the headline at every width from 1600 down. The cell, rings and all, is hidden below 1180 px. **Secondary ink is `--ink-2` / `--ink-3`, never `opacity`**: one alpha over two grounds is two contrast ratios, and ten captions failed AA on light while passing on dark from the same declaration. **`.reveal` is scoped to `html.js`** so the page's resting state is visible; see the Motion section of `DESIGN.md` for what moves and why. |
| `src/landingGlobe.ts` | **The hero globe.** A real WebGL sphere, idle-painted so first paint never waits on it, falling back to a still image where WebGL is refused. Coastlines and the study region come from the same manifest the platform reads, and the region is filled from the newest analysis's 5 m temperature Grid through `palette.ts`'s own `colourOf` and `liftedPalette` - so the globe and the platform's sheet cannot disagree about what a degree looks like. The fill is 56 x 36, one texel a degree, and **land texels carry the colour of the nearest water at zero alpha**: a transparent texel still has RGB and `LinearFilter` blends it, so leaving land at the array's initial black drew a grey fringe down every coast. |
| `provenance.html` | Data provenance. Every figure read live from the baked manifest; nothing hardcoded. |

| `src/store.ts` | Zustand store. `setBiasMode(true)` also moves the timeline to the newest analysis, where 92% of the bias map's markers sit at their own cast, so switching the mode on reads as the dots changing colour rather than jumping. | `src/types.ts` | Shapes of the baked JSON. Keep in step with `bake.py`. |
| `src/guide.ts` | **Plain-language explanation of every control.** Edit here to change what the guide panel says. | `src/floatTime.ts` | `positionAt()` / `trackUpTo()` - where a float was at a given moment. The window it uses is the bake's, read from the manifest. |
| `src/agreement.ts` | **How close is close.** The two fractions of a Field's own range at which the model stops agreeing with an instrument, and the position along the diverging palette a signed bias earns. Read by the Collocation verdict *and* by the bias map, so the list cannot call a float the worst in the basin while its own panel says "Close agreement". |
| `src/drift.ts` | The drift integrator **in the browser**, so a dropped pin works with the network unplugged and on the static deployment where there is no API. This is the project's one deliberate second implementation of a piece of science, and it is the *checked* kind: `pipeline/oceanverity/drift.py` is the tested one, and `probe-drift.mjs` runs this module from the baked start points and fails if the two disagree by more than 2 km. Measured: median 0.331 km, worst 1.573 km over 101 days. |
| `src/section.ts` | The vertical section, **cut in the browser** from the float32 Grid shipped in `public/data/grids/`, so it works offline and on the static deployment. | `src/palette.ts` | The display lift applied to cmocean palettes, **per theme**. | `src/surface.ts` | **The Sheet and the Drape's lattice, resampled once.** Bilinear on the *values* before anything is coloured - blurring colours would interpolate through the palette - and it returns a coverage fraction per cell, which is the alpha ramp that turned the 110 km coast from a cliff into a fade. Both render paths call it, so they cannot disagree about where the coast is. |
| `src/transfer.ts` | **The log/linear Scale, in one place**, plus the two questions that decide where it and the isosurface may act: `supportsLog` (a real zero, a gradient to bend, never negative) and `isDiverging` (a meaningful midpoint, so two isosurface skins and no log). A TypeScript function and the identical GLSL as a string; the shader inlines the string and the colourbar samples the function. There is deliberately no second copy - one is exactly what got this feature cut the first time. |
| `src/data/upload.ts` | **The only place in the frontend that talks to a server**, and it only ever does when a user drops a NetCDF file. Posts the raw bytes, carries the refusal back with the axis it names, and maps this platform's Timesteps onto the file's own instants. `API_BASE` is `VITE_API_BASE` or localhost:8000. |

| `src/scene/OceanScene.ts` | The largest file. Renderer, camera, all geometry, picking, the `ORDER` draw-order table, `debug()`. |
| `src/scene/volumeShader.ts` | The ray-marching GLSL. Transfer function, depth gate, isosurface, gradient emphasis, log scale. |
| `src/scene/fieldShaders.ts` | The two render types that are **not** a ray march: the Sheet a depth Field sits on, and the current arrows.
