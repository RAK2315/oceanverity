The September 2026 revision made this a Disaster Management problem. That is an opportunity rather than a difficulty, because the quantities a hazard forecaster names all fall out of the temperature and salinity this platform already reads.

Theme: Disaster Management Answered

INCOIS used to publish exactly the quantities a cyclone forecaster asks for - depth of the 26 °C isotherm, heat content, mixed layer depth, isothermal layer depth - in their value-added product series. That series stopped on 2019-03-30, measured twice and independently, on their ERDDAP and again in their LAS catalogue. All five are computed here from the temperature and salinity already in the grid, so this fills a gap INCOIS has rather than duplicating something they ship.

Three.js and WebGL2. The isosurface is named for the Field it cuts - an isotherm, an isohaline, an isopycnal - because a surface of constant value is a different object in each. The same block carries the current vectors the problem statement asks for next: Copernicus Marine's own analysis at one twelfth of a degree, read with a free account when the data is baked and never in the browser, drawn as arrows on the depth you have sliced to, coloured by speed, with a real number under the cursor.

Instrument overlay with geospatially accurate markers; click a float to inspect a depth-versus-variable profile chart with timestamps Argo, moorings and BGC met; gliders read; CTD refused

Every instrument is drawn where it actually was at the moment on screen, and is not drawn at all when its nearest cast is too far from that moment - showing it would imply an observation that does not exist. Multi-format ingestion: NetCDF via an xarray backend, and delimited text formats, with new sources added with minimal code change Met, and you can check it yourself

NetCDF through xarray and netCDF4, CSV through a parser whose column layout is stored as data rather than code, and the EGO glider directory index through a parser that reads its columns from the file's own header rather than by position. Then the part that can be falsified: drag a CF-conventions NetCDF file of your own onto the platform's Your own data panel and its variables appear in the Variable selector, going through the same colourbar, depth slice, isosurface and 3D renderer as everything else. It is read by a Source Adapter behind exactly the same interface as INCOIS, Argo and Copernicus. A file it cannot read names the axis and draws nothing: no longitude, a sigma-level vertical coordinate, an ensemble dimension, or an ocean somewhere else in the world are each refused in words. It is an API feature, like OPeNDAP and WMS - the demo runs with the API stopped.

Customizable colorbar: palette, min/max range, log/linear scale; variable selector; layer opacity control; vertical exaggeration slider Met

Both range handles, and the range is analytical rather than cosmetic - water outside it is not drawn at all. The log/linear toggle was cut once because the shader bent the water while the legend stayed straight; there is now exactly one curve, in one file, used by the ray marcher and the swatch alike. There is deliberately no palette chooser: each variable carries the cmocean scale designed for its quantity, because a chooser let a user put an oxygen scale on temperature. The variable selector carries five groups - most of them computed here rather than downloaded, which is the extensibility claim made visible: INCOIS publish two quantities and the platform offers many more from them - and the layer's opacity and the block's vertical exaggeration are each their own slider, because a lens and a read are different controls.

Modern JavaScript frontend, lightweight REST API backend, OPeNDAP API backend, deployable on INCOIS infrastructure with no client-side dependencies Met

TypeScript, React and Vite in front; FastAPI behind, serving DAP2, CF-1.8 NetCDF and OGC WMS from the native analysis grids and never from the rendering volume. The OPeNDAP endpoint is checked in the test suite by opening it with a real pydap client rather than by asserting on our own bytes.

docs/adr/0012
Extensible design for CTDs, moorings, HF-radar, ADCP and machine-learning derived products Moorings met, the rest answered

Moored buoys are wired up through NOAA's public GTS feed - a genuinely different format, with depth rather than pressure, one row per level and no quality flags, absorbed behind the same Protocol. HF-radar and ADCP stay unmet because India's are behind a login that does not resolve, which is a data-policy fact rather than an architecture gap. Machine-learning products are refused on purpose: thirty-six analyses is not a training set, and a gap-filler would paint smooth believable temperature over the part of the block nobody measured, which is the most honest thing in the tool.

Beyond the six core: the rest of the problem statement

Tools that enable intuitive and rapid understanding of three-dimensional ocean phenomena Met, and not asked for

Draw a line between two points on the water and the platform cuts a vertical section along it: depth down, distance across, the variable as colour, with every float cast within 150 km of the line on the same axes and each drawn down to the depth it actually reached. This is the standard figure of physical oceanography - the one in every textbook and every INCOIS report - and no interactive web tool offers it cut live against a 3D block you can also fly through. It reads the model's own grid on its own 24 levels, never the depth-warped rendering volume, because a reader takes metres off one axis and a value off the colour. The three variables an instrument measures ship their full-precision grids in the build, so it works with the network unplugged; /api/section serves the same cut to anything else.

The absence of such a system impedes timely ... climate monitoring Met

Two variables answer this and the difference between them is the point. The Temperature Anomaly is a departure from the mean of the thirty-six analyses in this build - a year, August 2025 to July 2026 - which is a seasonal swing, and the panel beside it has always said so. Temperature vs Normal is a departure from NOAA's World Ocean Atlas 2023 1991-2020 mean for that analysis's own calendar month, which is what a forecaster means by "warmer than usual". It is read anonymously over OPeNDAP at bake time, needs no account at any point, and sits on exactly the node centres INCOIS use - which is why no regridding happens and why the platform refuses outright rather than regridding if that ever stops being true. Below 1500 m the atlas has no normal, so the field is blank there rather than zero.

Open the climatological anomaly docs/adr/0016
The absence of such a system impedes timely hazard assessment, search-and-rescue support, fishery advisories and climate monitoring Search and rescue: built, and scored

This is not a search-and-rescue forecast and is never labelled as one. A real one needs surface wind, Stokes drift from the waves and a leeway coefficient for the specific object, and this carries none of them - which is why INCOIS run SARAT. What it shows is the drift the ocean analysis alone implies: drop a pin, and the Copernicus current field is integrated forward from it at the depth you have sliced to. What makes it worth having is that it checks itself. An Argo float's track is measured drift at its parking depth, so the same integrator was run from every drifting float's own position and the answer published, unflattering parts included.

Tools for intuitive, rapid understanding: where does the model disagree with the instruments? Met, and not asked for

The problem statement asks for a comparison. It does not ask for the comparison to be searchable, and clicking 266 instruments one at a time to find the three that matter is not rapid understanding. So every collocated instrument is also drawn coloured by how far the analysis sat from what it measured, ranked worst first, and binned onto 5 degree boxes so a regional bias is distinguishable from scatter. It is not AI. There is no model here, no training set and no confidence score: every figure is the mean or the root-mean-square of residuals already measured, and the ranking is on that value as a fraction of each variable's own range so a degree and a PSU can share one list.

Open standards (OGC WMS/WCS, CF Conventions for NetCDF) WMS, OPeNDAP and CF met; WCS declined with the reason

Three standards read the model back out: OGC WMS 1.3.0, DAP2 OPeNDAP and CF-1.8 NetCDF, all served from the native analysis grids and never from the depth-warped rendering volume - a consumer pulling data over the wire gets the same numbers the platform reports. The axis-order trap is the first thing tested: WMS 1.3.0 takes EPSG:4326 as latitude-first while CRS:84 is longitude-first, and a flipped axis would draw an image flipped north-south without ever failing. The OPeNDAP and WMS endpoints are checked by opening them with a real client (pydap and owslib) rather than by asserting on our own bytes. WCS is not served, on the measurement rather than the hardware: there is no maintained pure-Python WCS server, DAP2 is what this community actually reads, and the same coverages are already on OPeNDAP - so the refusal is written down here instead of reading as an omission.

Public outreach and science communication: school and college students, the general public during awareness campaigns, policymakers, outreach events, exhibitions, e-learning Four of the six, and the two gaps named

The problem statement gives this its own titled section and names five audiences and three settings by hand, and fifteen variables in five groups is the right toolkit for a forecaster and the wrong first minute for any of them. So there are two more doors, and the control panel gained nothing. Show me around is a guided walk in six chapters and 21 steps that visits every one of the 44 controls this platform explains - a probe fails if a control is ever added without a step, so that is a measurement rather than a promise. Explore is the same platform as eight questions, each of which sets up its own answer and each of which carries the caveat its simplification costs: the cyclone one says in as many words that it is a map of conditions and not a forecast. Kiosk mode hides the panels, scales the type and plays the questions on a loop, resetting thirty seconds after the last visitor walks away, which is what "exhibitions" actually asks for. Copy this view writes what is on screen into a link, which is a teacher's worksheet.

What is missing is stated rather than glossed: there is no printable one-page brief for the policymaker row, and the app carries one media query, at 1180 px, so a classroom of laptops is fine and a phone is not.
